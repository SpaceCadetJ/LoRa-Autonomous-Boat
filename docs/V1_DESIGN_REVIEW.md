# V1 design review — LoRa Autonomous Boat Controller (senior design v5.brd, as fabricated)

Written 2026-09-16 from the reconstructed board (`hardware/kicad/`), the v5 fabrication films, `pstxnet.dat`, the firmware, and the current datasheets. Every finding states its evidence. Severity: **Safety** (can hurt people or destroy the boat/board), **Functional** (a feature cannot work as designed), **Reliability** (works, but marginally or by luck), **Nice** (improvement). Finding IDs are referenced by `V2_REQUIREMENTS.md` and `REPORT.md`.

Evidence shorthand: `pstxnet` = `Allegro/hardware/allegro-original/Allegro v5/Allegro/pstxnet.dat`; `DS-*` = `docs/research/DATASHEET_NOTES.md` section; `FW §n` = `docs/research/FIRMWARE_REVIEW_NOTES.md` section; `main.c:n` = `firmware/Core/Src/main.c`; DRC/XOR numbers = `hardware/kicad/_build/*.json` regenerated by `build_v1.py`.

## 2. Application analysis

### 2.1 Link budget at 915 MHz

Propagation over water at low antenna heights is not free-space. Beyond the two-ray break distance d_c = 4·h₁·h₂/λ (5.5 m for h₁ = 0.3 m on the boat, h₂ = 1.5 m in hand, λ = 0.328 m) the path loss follows the two-ray ground-reflection model, PL = 40·log₁₀(d) − 20·log₁₀(h₁·h₂) dB, i.e. 126.9 dB at 1 km (free space would be 91.7 dB). Range therefore scales with the fourth root of link margin: +6 dB buys only 41 % more distance, and antenna height buys as much as transmit power.

Assumptions: RYLR998 +22 dBm (DS-6a), RYLR896 +15 dBm (DS-6b); receiver sensitivity per spreading factor from the Semtech SX126x/SX127x datasheet typicals at BW 125 kHz (SF7 −124, SF9 −129, SF10 −132, SF12 −137 dBm; the RYLR998 sheet quotes a single −129 dBm); module helical antennas at −1 dBi each end (gain unpublished; these are electrically short at 16 mm), or 2 dBi quarter-wave whips; 12 dB fade margin for wave-motion multipath and a wet hand on the handset.

| Module / SF | Sensitivity | Margin-limited path loss (dB) | Range, helical both ends | Range, 2 dBi whips both ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RYLR998 SF7 | −124 | 22 − 2 + 124 − 12 = 132 | 1.34 km | 1.9 km |
| RYLR998 SF9 | −129 | 137 | 1.79 km | 2.5 km |
| RYLR998 SF10 | −132 | 140 | 2.13 km | 3.0 km |
| RYLR998 SF12 | −137 | 145 | 2.84 km | 4.0 km |
| RYLR896 SF7 | −123 | 15 − 2 + 123 − 12 = 124 | 0.85 km | 1.2 km |
| RYLR896 SF12 | −137 | 138 | 1.9 km | 2.7 km |

Raising the handset antenna from 1.5 m to 2.5 m and the boat antenna from 0.3 m to 0.5 m adds 8.8 dB (×1.66 range) — more than the whole SF7→SF12 gap costs in data rate.

### 2.2 Air time and command rate (BW 125 kHz, CR 4/5, explicit header, CRC on)

Symbol time T_s = 2^SF / 125 kHz. The RYLR module adds 8 bytes to every payload. Preamble 4 symbols as the firmware sets (RYLR896); the RYLR998 default is 12.

| Packet | SF7 | SF9 | SF10 | SF12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `THRUST,50` (9 + 8 B) | 58 ms | 148 ms | 297 ms | 1.19 s |
| `GPS,42.123456,-83.123456` (24 + 8 B) | 88 ms | 230 ms | 420 ms | 1.68 s |
| Max command rate, one direction, no telemetry | 17 /s | 6.7 /s | 3.4 /s | 0.84 /s |
| Boat busy transmitting (telemetry every 5 s) | 1.8 % | 4.6 % | 8.4 % | 34 % |

V1 sends THRUST and RUDDER as two packets, so a full control update at SF12 takes ~2.4 s and collides with the 1.68 s telemetry burst a third of the time. A combined 8-byte command (thrust, rudder, arm, sequence, CRC) at SF7 costs ~55 ms and supports 10 Hz with telemetry at 1 Hz; at SF9 ~140 ms and 5 Hz.

**Recommended operating point for a boat with a joystick handset:** SF7 or SF8, BW 125 kHz, CR 4/5, combined command packet at 5-10 Hz, telemetry 1 Hz, 2 dBi external antennas on both ends with the handset antenna held high — about 2 km over water with 12 dB margin, 60 ms command latency. Fall back to SF9/SF10 automatically on RSSI (1 Hz command rate is still usable for a boat). SF12 with the module helical gives ~2.8 km but a >1 s control latency, which is the wrong trade for a vessel that needs to be steered.

**Module trade (decision 1):** keep the RYLR998 header (AT-command module, no RF layout risk, 22 dBm) versus an SX1262 module with u.FL (e.g. E22-900M22S, +22 dBm, ~ $5, needs a 50 Ω feed and an SPI driver): the discrete module gives full control of SF/BW/timing, real ACK/CAD, CRC and ~3 dB better sensitivity, at the cost of ~2 days of driver work. The recommended default is the RYLR998 (or RYLR998_M4 with I-PEX for an external antenna) on a proper keep-out with an SMA bulkhead, because it keeps the handset compatible.

### 2.3 Control loop

Heading hold and waypoint following need: a heading source at ≥ 20 Hz (IMU + magnetometer, tilt-compensated; GNSS course is invalid below ~1 m/s), position at ≥ 5 Hz with PPS, a control loop at 20-50 Hz, actuator update at 50 Hz (servo PWM) and a supervisor: command age > `T_loss` → throttle idle, rudder neutral; low battery → hold/return; watchdog reset → outputs safe before firmware. `T_loss` should be derived from the tolerable uncontrolled travel: at 3 m/s a 1 s timeout is 3 m. V2 requirements: REQ-CTL-01..06, REQ-NAV-01..05.

### 2.4 Power

Battery chemistry and cell count are not in the repo (decision 2; the V1 parts allow ≤ 21 V comfortably). 3.3 V rail: ≈ 15 mA MCU (16 MHz HSI) + 17.5 mA LoRa RX / 145 mA TX + ≤ 45 mA GNSS + ~5 mA IMU → 210 mA peak, 80-100 mA average → 1.2 A R1240N is adequate (and ~0.35 W dissipation at 12 V in is fine for the SOT-23-6W). Servo and ESC must get their own rail (5-6 V, 1-3 A, from the ESC's BEC or a dedicated buck) with no path into the logic rail; V1's +3V3 on the header power pins is the single most dangerous connection on the board after the missing failsafe. Input: fuse (3-5 A for the logic board only if the ESC is fed directly from the battery), reverse-polarity P-FET or Schottky, 33 V TVS, ≥ 47 µF bulk, and a battery terminal that fits the wire.

## 3. What V1 got right (keep in V2)

STM32F446 with the ARM 10-pin debug connector; the R1240N/CMS06/SLF7045 buck (once returned properly and with a fuse in front); the pi-filter on the LoRa supply; the copper keep-out under the LoRa module; test points on both PWM lines; the 2-layer 1.6 mm process; the JST XH for CAN. The LQFP fan-out at 7.7 mil spacing is a real fabricator constraint that V2 avoids by routing 6 mil.
